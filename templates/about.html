How a verdict is produced

  1. Tokenization. Your text is lower-cased and split into WordPiece tokens. The special tokens [CLS] and [SEP] mark the start and end. Anything past 512 tokens is cut off, and the result page tells you when that happens.
  2. Encoding. Twelve transformer layers build a contextual representation of every token, looking in both directions at once.
  3. Classification. A linear head reads the [CLS] vector and outputs two scores. Softmax turns them into probabilities that add up to 100%.
  4. Verdict. The higher probability becomes the label. Its value is the confidence you see. Anything under 70% is flagged as low confidence.

Training data

According to its model card, the classifier was fine-tuned from bert-base-uncased on the Pulk17 fake news dataset. Articles labeled real are largely Reuters wire reports on politics and world news. Articles labeled fake come from sites flagged for misinformation. Most of it is US political coverage from 2016–2017.

Limitations you should know
  • It classifies writing style. It does not verify facts, check sources or read links.
  • A false story written in a sober wire-service style can be labeled Real, and informal real reporting can be labeled Fake.
  • Accuracy drops on topics, periods and languages that differ from its training data.
  • High confidence is not proof. It only means the text looks very typical of one class.

Privacy

The model runs on the server hosting this app. Submitted text is stored in this installation's database so it can appear in your history, and is visible to its administrators. It isn't sent to any external AI service.
